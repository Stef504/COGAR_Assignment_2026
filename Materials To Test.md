## Materials to test:
1. ABS Plastic Object (i.e., Lego, 3D printed component) – With a 3D printed component, we can do a standard square and a complex shape to compare how identification is handled and a shape where the ends are curved and the rest is complex. Most of the research uses different types of plastics but combines them with visual sensors to identify the material, but they don’t use ground truths nor set a standard for a specific type of plastic because, for example, ABS has a different hardness vs PET vs PP and therefore different grasping forces and organisation techniques. 
    a. Objects: `3D Printed` block and a 3D printed star. 
       Printing Material used: ABS.

2. Glass: Most glass objects have pretty standard shapes (i.e., in industry uses we have bottled glass, curved, but mostly a flat surface), so geometry shouldn’t be a problem with material identification. 
    a. `Coce-Cola` glass bottle or glass water bottle

3. A wooden block, circular. The idea is to take commonly shaped wooden objects where the sensor will likely be used, and since in the environment there isn’t too complex a shape of wood, it would be better to set the standard for the project to what is known first. Then we can use natural wood (no surface finish, etc.) for environmental searching.
    a. `Kids wooden blocks`. Standard square and maybe a star for the complex shape. 

4. Rubber ball: Here we can compare Young's modulus and Shore hardness to correctly set the benchmark. 
    a. `Rubber ball`. (Found in lab)

5. Metallic Part: To do a standard benchmark test, we can use either aluminium or steel. Which are commonly used in factories, i.e., bolts, screws,nuts , tin cans, frames, and bodies. 
    a. `Aluminium can and a bolt` (steel 304)

6.  Combination of metal and plastic - this is for a multilevel material classification. Since we start with the benchmarks, we should see maybe some similar comparisons. 
    a. Take the metallic parts and superglue a similar 3D-printed component so we can have a continuous geometry. 

7. Could do a wooden piece with a 3D print attached to it so we have a combination of wood and ABS plastic and are adding to multilevel material classifications. 
    a. With respect to points 5 and 6, we can make it so one sensor touches the one material and the other material. 
    b. We can test again where both sensors test the combination of the materials, so i.e., wood and plastic integration is being touched by the sensor…
